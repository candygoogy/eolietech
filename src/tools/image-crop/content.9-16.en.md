## Stories, Reels, TikTok and Shorts

All of these fill a phone screen held upright, in a 9:16 frame of 1080 × 1920 px. When you post a picture of another shape, the app either zooms in and cuts off the edges, or leaves a band of colour around it. Cropping to 9:16 yourself means you choose what fills the screen, and the same picture works in all four apps.

## Cropping a photo to 9:16

9:16 is narrower than almost any camera photo, so the crop mostly removes the sides. From an upright phone photo (3:4), a full-height 9:16 crop drops a quarter of the width, which is usually just background. From a landscape photo it keeps less than half the width, so drag the box sideways until the person or the object you care about sits inside it. To post the whole landscape photo, [Fit an image to 9:16](/fit-image-to-9-16/) puts it in a 9:16 frame with a blurred or plain background.

While the ratio is turned, the other buttons are turned too and read 5:4, 4:3 and 2:3. Press **Rotate ratio** to bring back 16:9 and the usual 4:5, 3:4 and 3:2. For a wide crop from the start, [Crop to 16:9](/crop-image-16-9/) opens the other way round.

To bring the result to 1080 × 1920 px, use [Resize images for Instagram](/resize-image-for-instagram/), which sets the width to 1080 px and keeps the shape. For feed posts rather than Stories, [Crop a photo for Instagram](/crop-image-for-instagram/) opens at 4:5.

## Phone wallpapers

A 9:16 image exactly fills older phones such as the iPhone 8, but most phones from the last few years are taller, around 9:19.5 or 9:20. On those, the phone zooms in and trims the sides of a 9:16 wallpaper. For an exact fit, choose **Free** and type your screen's width and height in pixels: you get exactly that size, as long as the image is at least that big.

## How it works

Your image is decoded in a background worker inside this tab, and the 9:16 area is cut from the full-resolution pixels, not from the smaller preview. Phone photos are turned upright first, using the orientation saved with them, so the crop matches what you see. The result is saved as JPG, PNG, WebP or AVIF without the original's EXIF data, such as GPS location. Nothing is sent over the network.
