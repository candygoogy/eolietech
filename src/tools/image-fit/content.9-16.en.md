## Landscape photos in Stories and Reels

A Story, a Reel or a TikTok fills a phone held upright, in a 9:16 frame of 1080 × 1920 px. Almost every photo is wider than that. A group shot, a panorama or a wide screenshot loses the very parts you wanted to show when the app zooms in to fill the screen. Fitting the photo here first puts the whole picture in the Story, and you choose what fills the rest of the screen.

## Which size to use

With **Fixed width** at 1080, every photo comes out at 1080 × 1920 px, ready for Stories, Reels, TikTok and Shorts. An upright phone photo (3:4) becomes 1080 × 1440 px inside that frame, with a band of 240 px above and below. A square photo gets bands of 420 px.

**Same as the photo** keeps the photo at full size and makes the frame taller around it: a 4032 × 3024 px landscape photo becomes 4032 × 7168 px. That is a large file the apps scale down anyway, so Fixed width is the better choice here.

## Which background to use

**Blurred photo** is a common choice for Stories: the bands take on the photo's colours, darkened a little so the photo stands out. **Black** suits night shots and dark photos. **White** or a **Colour** suits a quote, a product shot or a poster that needs a clean edge.

The photo always sits in the middle. To fill the screen with part of the photo, use [Crop to 9:16](/crop-image-9-16/). For feed posts, [Make an image square](/make-image-square/) has 1:1 and 4:5, and for a wide version of the same photo, [Fit an image to 16:9](/fit-image-to-16-9/).

## How it works

Each photo is decoded in a background worker inside this tab and turned upright first, so a photo taken with the phone on its side ends up the right way up in the frame. If you change the shape, background or size after adding photos, the ones in the list are made again with the new settings, so you can try Blurred photo and Black and compare the thumbnails. The files are saved without EXIF data, and nothing leaves your device.
